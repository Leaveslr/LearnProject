## Raw Question

Why do single-factor backtests often look strong in notebooks but fail after transaction costs, universe changes, and live execution?

## Initial Notes

- A factor can look predictive because the test accidentally uses future information.
- Survivorship bias can remove failed companies from history.
- High turnover can erase the apparent alpha after fees and slippage.
- A notebook-level result is not yet a tradable strategy.
